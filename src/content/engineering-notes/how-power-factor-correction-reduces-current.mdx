A 100 kW load is operating from a 600 V, three-phase supply at 0.80 lagging power factor. After a capacitor bank raises the source power factor to 0.95 lagging, the source current falls from approximately **120.3 A to 101.3 A**.

The real power is still 100 kW. The load itself has not changed. So where did the current go?

The answer is in the reactive-current path. The shunt capacitor supplies part of the load's reactive current locally, so less reactive current has to travel through the upstream source and conductors.

<Callout type="important-note" title="The central engineering point">
**Power factor correction reduces the current supplied by the source, not the current drawn by the unchanged inductive load.**
</Callout>

## The System Being Compared

The example uses a balanced, three-phase inductive load under constant operating conditions:

- Line-to-line voltage: **600 V AC**
- Frequency: **60 Hz**
- Real power: **100 kW**
- Initial load power factor: **0.80 lagging**
- Corrected source power factor: **0.95 lagging**
- Compensation: a shunt capacitor bank connected in parallel with the load

The load, supply voltage, and real power do not change when the capacitor is connected. Only the source-side reactive-power demand changes.

## Before Power Factor Correction

For a balanced three-phase system, line current is:

<EquationBlock label="1">
$$
I=\frac{P}{\sqrt{3}\,V_{LL}\,PF}
$$
</EquationBlock>

At 0.80 power factor:

<EquationBlock label="2">
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{source,before}
&=\frac{100{,}000}{\sqrt{3}\times600\times0.80}\\
&\approx120.3\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
</EquationBlock>

The apparent power is:

<EquationBlock label="3">
$$
S_{before}=\frac{P}{PF}=\frac{100}{0.80}=125\,\mathrm{kVA}
$$
</EquationBlock>

Reactive power is found from:

<EquationBlock label="4">
$$
Q=P\tan\!\left(\cos^{-1}(PF)\right)
$$
</EquationBlock>

Therefore:

<EquationBlock label="5">
$$
Q_{before}=100\tan\!\left(\cos^{-1}(0.80)\right)=75.0\,\mathrm{kVAr}
$$
</EquationBlock>

Before correction, the source supplies all 100 kW of real power and all 75.0 kVAr of inductive reactive power. The source current and load current are therefore the same: approximately 120.3 A.

## What Changes When the Capacitor Bank Is Connected?

The capacitor bank is connected in parallel with the inductive load. It does not sit in series with the load and does not directly force the load current to a lower value.

Instead, the capacitor draws a leading current. Its reactive component opposes part of the load's lagging reactive current at the point of connection.

The source still supplies the full **100 kW** required by the load. However, it now supplies only the portion of inductive reactive power that remains after the capacitor contribution is included.

This is why three currents must be kept distinct:

- **Load current:** remains approximately 120.3 A because the load operating point is unchanged.
- **Capacitor current:** a leading branch current that provides capacitive reactive power locally.
- **Source current:** the phasor sum of the load and capacitor branch currents; its reactive component is smaller after correction.

These RMS current magnitudes should not be added or subtracted arithmetically. Voltage-aligned and quadrature components must be combined as phasors.

## After Correction to 0.95 Lagging

At a corrected source power factor of 0.95:

<EquationBlock label="6">
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{source,after}
&=\frac{100{,}000}{\sqrt{3}\times600\times0.95}\\
&\approx101.3\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
</EquationBlock>

The source apparent power becomes:

<EquationBlock label="7">
$$
S_{after}=\frac{100}{0.95}\approx105.3\,\mathrm{kVA}
$$
</EquationBlock>

The remaining reactive power supplied from upstream is:

<EquationBlock label="8">
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{after}
&=100\tan\!\left(\cos^{-1}(0.95)\right)\\
&\approx32.9\,\mathrm{kVAr}
\end{aligned}
$$
</EquationBlock>

The required capacitor compensation is the difference:

<EquationBlock label="9">
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_c&=Q_{before}-Q_{after}\\
&=75.0-32.9\\
&\approx42.1\,\mathrm{kVAr}
\end{aligned}
$$
</EquationBlock>

An ideal 42.1 kVAr, 600 V three-phase capacitor bank corresponds to approximately 40.5 A of line current in its branch. That leading current cancels part of the load's lagging reactive-current component at the common connection point.

The source current falls by approximately **19.0 A**, or **15.8%**, while the source continues to deliver the same 100 kW. The source apparent power decreases by the same percentage because the voltage is fixed.

## Why the Source Current Moves Closer to the Voltage

At 0.80 lagging power factor, the source current lags the source voltage by approximately 36.9°. Its reactive component is relatively large.

After correction to 0.95 lagging, the source current lags by only about 18.2°. The current phasor has moved closer to the voltage phasor because the capacitor supplies part of the quadrature current locally.

The in-phase current component associated with real power remains essentially unchanged. What decreases is the net lagging reactive component seen by the source. With a smaller quadrature component, the total source-current magnitude is lower.

## Practical Engineering Considerations

The 42.1 kVAr result is the ideal compensation required at this one operating point. A practical capacitor-bank design needs more than a single steady-state calculation.

Capacitor banks must be selected and applied with care when:

- the load varies enough to require stepped or automatically controlled compensation;
- harmonic-producing loads are present;
- the capacitor bank and system inductance could create a resonant condition;
- switching transients, discharge time, or inrush current affect the switching equipment;
- the installation could become overcorrected and operate at leading power factor during light load.

In systems with significant harmonics, a harmonic study may be needed. Detuned reactors or another purpose-designed solution may be appropriate; a standard capacitor bank should not be applied solely from the fundamental-frequency kVAr calculation.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not confuse lower current with lower real energy">
Power factor correction does not reduce the unchanged load's 100 kW real-power demand. It may reduce upstream $I^2R$ losses and can affect utility demand or power-factor charges, but it does not guarantee a lower electricity bill. The result depends on the installation and tariff.
</Callout>

## Assumptions and Limits

This example assumes:

- balanced three-phase operation;
- sinusoidal voltage and current;
- constant load and constant 600 V supply voltage;
- idealized shunt compensation with negligible losses;
- no harmonic distortion; and
- unchanged load impedance and operating conditions.

Real installations should be evaluated using measured or manufacturer-provided load data, actual voltage, system impedance, load profile, harmonic spectrum, equipment ratings, and applicable codes and utility requirements.

## Key Takeaway

The capacitor bank does not make the unchanged inductive load draw less current. It creates a second branch that supplies part of the required reactive current near the load.

As a result, the upstream source supplies the same real power but less reactive power. The source current becomes more closely aligned with the voltage and falls from approximately **120.3 A to 101.3 A**.
